TL;DR

AIMAG upscales and denoises low-dose X-ray and fluoroscopy frames in real time inside the cath-lab. I designed the model, drove quantization, and built the C++ serving layer — taking inference from a research prototype to 600+ FPS on an RTX 4090 at <5% quality loss and 35% lower VRAM, then shipped it through clinical validation.

The problem

Interventional cardiology runs on X-ray fluoroscopy at very low dose — frames are noisy and small. Radiologists and cardiologists need sharper, larger, cleaner images without extra radiation to the patient or extra latency in the workflow. Off-the-shelf upscalers are too slow for live loops and lose diagnostically important detail when naively quantized.

What I built

1. Model R&D

A multi-scale super-resolution & denoising network trained on a curated clinical dataset. The architecture balances receptive field against real-time budget — deep enough to recover vessel edges and stent struts, shallow enough to hit a per-frame latency target in the cath-lab loop.

  • Curated and augmented the training data; built the eval protocol around perceptual + clinical metrics, not just PSNR/SSIM.
  • Tuned the loss to preserve high-frequency detail (vessel edges, devices) rather than over-smoothing.

2. Optimization & quantization

This is where the product became real. I built the full PyTorch → ONNX → TensorRT path with INT8/FP16 quantization, including a calibration pipeline that protected diagnostically critical regions from accuracy collapse.

Result: 600+ FPS on a single RTX 4090, a 70% latency reduction, multi-scale upsampling at <5% quality loss, and 35% lower VRAM — enough headroom to run alongside the rest of the imaging pipeline on the same workstation.

3. Serving stack

Production serving in C++ with FastAPI on the boundary, wired into the cath-lab's image acquisition loop. The pipeline is deterministic, observable, and restartable — designed to be validated for clinical use.

4. QCA — automatic vessel measurement

Beyond enhancement, I built an automatic Quantitative Coronary Analysis pipeline as a multi-stage deep-learning system: segmentation → calibration → prediction, delivering stenosis and vessel measurements in <4 seconds per frame series — work that directly underpins the segmentation approach in my open-source ArterySeg.

5. Team & clinical release

I led a 6-engineer ML team through the full lifecycle — roadmap, code-review standards, infra, and the clinical validation push that turned a research prototype into a billable product line.

What I learned

Quantization is not a post-training checkbox — protecting the right signal under INT8 is a modeling decision, not just a calibration trick. And in medical AI, the path to revenue runs through clinical validation: designing the eval to match how clinicians actually read images is what separates a demo from a product.
